Estimate vs Actual Job Cost Calculator

Margin fade is the gap between the margin you bid and the margin the job actually kept. Enter the bid, the final price, and your estimated and actual costs by category. The calculator shows the points lost to each cost line and to any price change. Free, runs in your browser, no sign-up.

Formula. Margin fade (points) = estimated margin % minus actual margin %. Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price. Each cost line adds (actual minus estimated) divided by the final price.

Example. Bid $18,500 with estimated costs of $15,050 gives an 18.6% margin. Final price $18,500 with actual costs of $17,050 gives 7.8%. The fade is 10.8 points: materials added $900 (4.9 points) and labor added $1,100 (5.9 points). Profit fell from $3,450 to $1,450.

How the numbers work

  • Estimated margin = (bid price minus estimated cost) divided by the bid price.
  • Actual margin = (final price minus actual cost) divided by the final price the customer paid.
  • Points lost to a cost line = (actual minus estimated for that line) divided by the final price.
  • Points from a price change = the rest of the fade. A higher final price makes this part negative, which means the price covered some of the overrun.
  • Negative inputs count as 0. A job that loses money shows a negative actual margin.

What this does not count

  • Overhead that is not part of the job. Enter it as an other job cost only if you charge it to the job.
  • Taxes, card fees and warranty callbacks, unless you enter them as costs.
  • Costs for work you did not price in the bid and did not enter as an actual cost.

Frequently asked questions

What is margin fade?

Margin fade is the number of points the job margin dropped between the bid and the finished job. A bid at 18.6% that finishes at 7.8% faded 10.8 points.

Which numbers do I need?

The bid price, your estimated cost for each category (materials, labor, subcontractors and other job costs), the final price the customer paid, and the actual cost for each category when the job is closed. Use the same categories on both sides.

Why does the result split into cost points and price points?

Cost points show which category took the margin. Price points show the effect of a final price that is higher or lower than the bid. The two parts add up to the fade.

What if the customer paid more because of a change order?

Enter the final price in total, including the approved added work. The price points then show how much of the overrun the extra price covered.

Does it work for a job that lost money?

Yes. A loss shows as a negative actual margin, and the fade can be large. The calculator does not stop at zero.

Does ClearDesk IQ do this for me?

The Windows app compares estimate and actual job costs by cost category, using the original line budgets and the expenses you record. Approved employee time is included in labor. The help article "Compare estimate and actual job costs" explains the screens.

Are the numbers I type stored or sent?

No. The page runs in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.
