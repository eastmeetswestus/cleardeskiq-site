Contractor Hourly Rate Calculator

Your hourly rate has to cover the pay you want, the tax on it and the overhead of running the business, spread over the hours you can actually bill. Enter those and the hours you work. The handyman and electrician examples fill the fields with fictional numbers. Free, runs in your browser, no sign-up.

Formula. Hourly rate = (owner pay grossed up for tax + yearly overhead) divided by billable hours. Grossed-up pay = owner pay divided by (1 minus tax %). Billable hours = hours a week times (52 minus weeks off) times billable %.

Example. Handyman: owner pay $60,000, overhead $18,000, 15.3% tax, 40 hours a week, 3 weeks off, 65% billable. Annual cost $88,838 divided by 1,274 billable hours is $69.73 an hour. Dividing by 2,080 hours gives $42.71, which leaves $27 an hour unrecovered.

How the numbers work

  • Owner pay is what you want to keep. Tax is added with the simplified tax percentage: owner pay divided by (1 minus the tax %).
  • Overhead is the yearly cost of running the business that is not billed to a job.
  • Billable hours = hours a week times working weeks times the billable share.
  • The 2,080 method divides by 40 hours for 52 weeks. It assumes every hour is billed, so it understates the rate.

What this does not count

  • Materials, which you price on each job.
  • Crew wages and their payroll costs, which belong in job costs.
  • Your own tax situation. The tax percentage is one editable figure, not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a contractor charge per hour?

There is no single right number. Your rate is the figure that covers your owner pay, tax and overhead over the hours you can bill. Enter your own numbers to find it.

Why not divide by 2,080 hours?

2,080 is 40 hours times 52 weeks. Most contractors cannot bill every hour: time off, estimating, travel, callbacks and shop work are not billed. Dividing by billable hours gives the rate you need.

What tax percentage should I use?

The default 15.3% is the combined self-employment rate: 12.4% Social Security on net earnings up to the yearly wage base, plus 2.9% Medicare on 92.35% of net earnings. The calculator uses one editable percentage, so enter your own figure from a tax professional.

What goes into overhead?

Yearly costs of running the business that are not on a job: insurance, tools, phone, software, office, marketing and licenses, and vehicle costs for non-job driving. The Windows app keeps these in its company overhead section.

How do I use the rate on a job?

Use it as your labor cost per hour when you price an estimate or a job.

What is the billable share?

The share of your working hours you can invoice a customer for. Start from your last few months: billed job hours divided by total work hours, then test it.

Is this tax or accounting advice?

No. It is an estimate from the numbers you enter.

Are the numbers I type stored or sent?

No. The page runs in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.
