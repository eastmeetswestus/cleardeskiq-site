Food Truck Break-Even Calculator

Break-even is the number of orders that covers your fixed costs for the month. Each order adds its sale minus food, packaging and card fees. Enter your monthly fixed costs and your per-order numbers to see the orders you need a day and the hours that takes at your pace. Free, runs in your browser, no sign-up.

Formula. Contribution per order = average sale minus food and packaging per order minus card and delivery fees. Break-even orders a month = fixed costs a month divided by the contribution per order. Orders a day = that figure divided by open days.

Example. Fixed costs $6,000 a month, average sale $12, food and packaging $3.80, fees 3%. Contribution is $7.84 an order, so break-even is 766 orders a month, about 30 a day. At 6 orders an hour that is 5 hours of service a day.

How the numbers work

  • Contribution per order is what each order adds toward fixed costs after food, packaging and fees.
  • Orders are rounded up to whole orders. Break-even sales = break-even orders times the average sale.
  • Hours of service = orders a day divided by the orders you expect per hour. If that is more than the hours you are open, the day cannot break even at that pace.
  • If the contribution is zero or negative, no number of orders covers fixed costs.

What this does not count

  • Your own pay, unless you put it in the fixed costs.
  • Waste, comps, spoilage and food you give away, beyond the per-order food cost you enter.
  • Sales tax. Enter the sale before tax.
  • Seasonal swings, events and catering.

Frequently asked questions

What is break-even for a food truck?

Break-even is the number of orders that covers all fixed costs for a month. Below it you lose money after fixed costs; above it the profit from each order pays for the truck, insurance and permits.

Which costs are fixed?

Costs that do not change with each order: the truck payment or lease, insurance, permits and license fees, commissary rent, and phone or software. Add owner pay if you want the truck to cover it.

Which average sale should I use?

Use your average check over a typical week. Run the calculator again with your best and slowest weeks to see the range.

How do I enter card and delivery fees?

As a percent of sales. A 3% fee on a $12 sale is $0.36, which the calculator subtracts from the contribution.

What if the contribution is zero or negative?

Then each order does not cover its own food, packaging and fees, and no number of orders covers fixed costs. Raise the price, lower the food cost per order, or change the menu before you add days.

How many hours do I need?

Hours needed = daily break-even orders divided by the orders you expect each hour. If that is more than the hours you are open, the day needs a higher price, lower costs or more sales per hour.
