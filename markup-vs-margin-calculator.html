The formulas

  • Markup = (price minus cost) divided by cost.
  • Margin = (price minus cost) divided by price.
  • Price for a target margin = cost divided by (1 minus the margin). A margin must be below 100%.
  • Price for a target markup = cost times (1 plus the markup).
  • Rounded up to the next cent, the way ClearDesk IQ prices an estimate.

A worked example

A cost of $100 sold at $150 is a 50% markup and a 33.33% margin. To earn a 30% margin on the same cost, the price is $142.86. A 25% markup gives $125.00, which is a 20% margin.

Why the two differ

Markup is measured on what you paid. Margin is measured on what the customer pays. The same $50 profit on a $100 cost is a 50% markup but only a 33% margin.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between markup and margin?

Markup is profit as a percent of cost. Margin is profit as a percent of price. A $100 cost sold at $150 is a 50% markup and a 33.33% margin.

Which one should I quote?

Quote the number your business plans around. Many contractors set prices on a target margin, because margin shows the share of each dollar you keep.

What is a 25% markup as a margin?

A 25% markup is a 20% margin on the same cost: profit of $25 on $125 of price.

Why is the price for a target margin cost divided by (1 minus margin)?

Because profit is taken from the price. To keep 30% of the price, the cost must be 70% of the price, so the price is cost divided by 0.70.

What does a margin of 100% mean?

It would need a price with no cost, so no price is defined. The calculator asks for a margin below 100%.

Can I use this for a whole job?

Use the job profitability calculator for a whole job with labor, overhead and other costs. Use this page to convert one cost and one price.

Is the markup on cost before or after overhead?

The calculator uses the cost you enter. Put overhead in that cost if you want the markup to include it.

Are the numbers I type stored or sent?

No. The page runs in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.
