How the numbers work

  • Usable cost of an ingredient = amount used, at the price you paid per unit, divided by the yield (for example, 85% yield means you keep 85% of what you buy).
  • Plate cost = the sum of the usable ingredient costs, divided by the portions the recipe makes, plus the flat costs per plate.
  • Food cost % = plate cost divided by the selling price, times 100.
  • Margin per plate = selling price minus plate cost.
  • Price to hit your target = plate cost divided by your target food cost %, optionally rounded up.
  • Rounded up to the next cent, the way ClearDesk IQ prices an estimate.

A worked example

The example above: chicken 8 oz at $3.50 per lb ($1.75), rice 4 oz at $1.20 per lb ($0.30), vegetables 5 oz at $2.40 per lb ($0.75), and $0.60 of sauce, garnish and container. Plate cost is $3.40. At a $14.00 price, food cost is 24.29% and the margin is $10.60. To hit 30%, the price is $11.34.

What this does not count

  • Labor, rent, utilities and insurance.
  • Waste you do not record as yield loss, and spills or comped plates.
  • Sales tax and card fees.

Frequently asked questions

What is plate cost?

Plate cost is what one dish costs in ingredients: the amount used at the price you paid, divided by the yield, summed for each ingredient and divided by the portions the recipe makes. Flat costs such as sauce and container are added.

What is a good food cost %?

Vendor guides commonly cite 28% to 35% of the menu price. Pick the target your concept needs and check it against your own prices.

What is yield?

The share of an ingredient you keep after trimming and waste. An 85% yield means you keep 85% of what you buy, so the cost of usable food is higher.

Why do the units have to match?

The calculator converts weight with weight, volume with volume and each with each. A pack in pounds cannot price an amount in cups, so that row is left out with a message.

What does food cost % leave out?

Labor, rent, utilities, insurance, waste you do not record, comps and spills, sales tax and card fees. Use the prime cost calculator for labor.

How do I use the price to hit target?

It is the plate cost divided by your target food cost %. Round up with the option you choose, so the food cost stays at or under the target.

Does ClearDesk IQ Restaurant track plate cost?

Yes. The Restaurant Edition builds a dish from ingredient prices and shows plate cost, food cost % and profit per plate, and it records vendor price changes in price history.

Are the numbers I type stored or sent?

No. The page runs in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.
