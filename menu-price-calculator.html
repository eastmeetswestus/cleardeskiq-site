Menu Price Calculator

Set a menu price from the plate cost of a dish and the food cost percentage you want. The calculator rounds up, so the dish stays at or under your target. Enter the current price to see whether the dish is on target. Free, runs in your browser, no sign-up.

Formula. Menu price = plate cost divided by target food cost %, rounded up to the cent or to the next $0.25 or $0.50. Food cost % = plate cost divided by menu price.

Example. Plate cost $3.40 at a 30% target: $3.40 divided by 0.30 is $11.33, rounded up to $11.34, or $11.50 if you round up to the next $0.25. At $11.50 the food cost is 29.57% and the profit per plate is $8.10.

How the numbers work

  • Plate cost comes from the food cost calculator: ingredient cost per portion plus flat costs such as sauce, garnish and container.
  • Rounding up keeps the food cost at or under the target. Rounding down pushes it over.
  • Enter menu prices before tax, the same way the Restaurant Edition does.
  • Profit per plate = menu price minus plate cost.

What this does not count

  • Labor, rent and other costs. See the prime cost calculator for labor.
  • Menu balance. A high-margin dish can still sell poorly, so check what sells.
  • Discounts, comps and combos, unless you price each one as its own dish.

Frequently asked questions

How do I price a menu item from food cost?

Divide the plate cost by your target food cost %. Then round up to a price that keeps the food cost at or under the target.

What food cost % should I target?

Vendor guides commonly cite 28% to 35% of the menu price. Use the target you already use, or test one target on a few dishes and compare the results.

Why round up?

Rounding up keeps the food cost at or under the target. Rounding down pushes it over the target.

Does the calculator include sales tax?

No. Enter menu prices before tax.

What if my plate cost is zero?

The calculator needs a plate cost above $0 to set a price. Add the ingredient costs first, using the food cost calculator.

What is the difference between food cost % and profit per plate?

Food cost % is plate cost as a share of the price. Profit per plate is the price minus plate cost. At the same price, a lower food cost % means more profit per plate.

Does ClearDesk IQ Restaurant Edition track this?

The Restaurant Edition shows each dish with its plate cost, food cost % and profit per plate, ranks dishes by profit per plate and flags dishes over their food cost target.

Are the numbers I type stored or sent?

No. The page runs in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent.
